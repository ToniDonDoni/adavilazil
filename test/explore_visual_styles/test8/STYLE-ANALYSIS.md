## Artists and purpose

1. **Gustave Dore (1832-1883), FS-065, nineteenth century:** source of extreme scale, directional light, engraved tonal depth and the human act of exploring an unknown world. [Reference](../../../reference/visual_styles/dore_dante_engraving/README.md).
2. **Karl Sims (Panspermia, 1990), FS-253, late twentieth century:** source of the concept of procedural emergence, variation, branching life, propagation and generated complexity. [Reference](../../../reference/visual_styles/sims_panspermia_procedural_life/README.md).

Both were absent from the existing reference catalog before this change. This pairing broadens the catalog beyond its existing Hiroshige, Kruger and Nees entries.

## Compatibility and conflicts

- **Compatible:** Dore's unexplored monumental space provides a landscape in which Sims's generative systems can grow. Dore's small explorer gives the complex results a human point of scale.
- **Tonal engraving versus procedural color:** keep the broad terrain ink-like, with restrained spectral accents only on newly generated life and discovery-light. This is an intentional contemporary hybrid, not a claim about original works.
- **Deliberate composition versus algorithmic emergence:** retain one deliberately legible path from seed through branching forms, while letting local structures vary.
- **Static poster versus animated source:** show stages of propagation in one spatial image. Do not claim motion was rendered.
- **Copyright/originality:** avoid references to any specific plate, movie frame, character, or recognizable morphology. The modern artist informs concepts only.
