## Exact generation prompt

Create an ORIGINAL fine-art poster, vertical portrait 2:3, titled exactly 'AI-Free Programming: Beyond the Algorithm'. This is not a copy, remake, movie still, fan art or imitation of a living artist. It merges two GENERAL artistic principles: dramatic sublime spatial exploration from nineteenth-century black-and-white literary engravings, and late-twentieth-century computational emergence, variation and artificial ecology. Invent all visual forms and arrangements.

A lone human investigator, visibly tiny in scale, stands at the edge of a monumental invented geological landscape. The person holds one small abstract code seed: a simple designed pattern, not readable software syntax. Trace a continuous visual transformation from this seed into a physically connected branching ecosystem of original, unfamiliar mineral-organic organisms. The ecological network grows into the middle and far distance, matching the terrain's perspective. Show several discernibly different branches and a readable shift from simple shapes near the source to varied complex forms deeper in the environment. This is a metaphor for programming used as an instrument for investigating nature and making possible worlds, not programming as delivery of corporate applications.

Use an immense deep-space scene with foreground rock, intermediate valleys and far luminous threshold. Strong dark ink-like tonal masses; an illuminated opening across the horizon; fine directional hatch-like line texture in selected geological surfaces. The environment is dominantly ivory and graphite, with restrained teal and pale-gold accents only where new hypothetical life emerges. Every part belongs to one spatially consistent world; no split panels, collage frames, charts, software interfaces, robots, brands or products.

Integrate a small, clear, original editorial title lockup with the exact text:
AI-Free Programming:
Beyond the Algorithm

Use ordinary unornamented contemporary lettering, well separated from the central growth and not copied from any recognizable poster design. Spell every letter correctly, no other text, no watermark. Aim for a museum-quality original allegory of exploration and worldmaking rather than a technological advertisement.
