# Style Analysis

**Skill:** EXPLORE-VISUAL-STYLES 2.3.0  
**Specification:** [SPEC-005-VOICE-ACROSS-THE-CITY.md](SPEC-005-VOICE-ACROSS-THE-CITY.md)

## Subject

One fictional adult female TV presenter with blonde hair, grey eyes, a dark tailored jacket and a visible lapel microphone. The same simplified face, hair silhouette and eye color are reused in every study. The on-air context is communicated by the microphone, presenter desk and a small ON AIR cartouche.

## Selected styles

- **Hiroshige's One Hundred Famous Views of Edo:** vertical print composition, oversized cropped foreground motif, unusual framing, layered city and river depth, flat color planes, selective bokashi and crisp woodblock contours.
- **Henryk Tomaszewski's Polish film posters:** concise visual metaphor instead of literal scene illustration, asymmetrical poster composition, expressive hand-drawn marks, purposeful color and a strong focal silhouette.

## Compatibility

Both references support a compact portrait composition and a single dominant focal idea. Hiroshige supplies the spatial grammar: the cropped blossom branch, layered city, river and bridge, flat printed color and atmospheric gradation. Tomaszewski supplies the poster logic: the image is reduced to one readable metaphor and one expressive vermilion gesture.

The shared metaphor is **voice becomes a bridge**. The presenter's lapel microphone is the origin of a vermilion signal ribbon. The ribbon travels across the lower composition and resolves into a bridge-like curve over the river, connecting the foreground presenter with the distant city.

## Conflicts and resolution

- **Crisp woodblock contours vs. painterly marks:** keep landscape and figure contours crisp; confine expressive brushwork to the single red signal ribbon and a few dry-brush accents.
- **Landscape detail vs. poster shorthand:** use the city, river and mountain as a small number of flat, readable planes rather than a dense scenic panorama.
- **Large foreground motif vs. presenter readability:** crop the blossom branch into the upper-left frame, but keep it outside the face silhouette.
- **Expressive red gesture vs. clear subject:** keep the ribbon below the eyes and face. It may cross the lower jacket and water, but it must begin at the microphone and remain visually continuous.
- **Bokashi vs. flat color:** restrict graded transitions to sky and water. Keep the face, clothing, branch and buildings in distinct printed color areas.

The first hybrid failed the integration rule because the red gesture floated above the presenter. V2 fixes the specific relationship rather than adding more decoration.
