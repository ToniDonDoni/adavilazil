# René Lalique: Dragonfly Jewellery and Translucent Enamel

## Overview
René Lalique's Art Nouveau jewellery (1890s–1900s) combined fauna, botanical forms and imaginative hybrids with gold, gemstones and translucent/opalescent enamel. The *Dragonfly-woman* corsage ornament (c. 1897–1898) is an emblematic museum-held example. Backlog FS-225. This entry abstracts techniques; it does not reproduce the object.

## Influenced By
- Art Nouveau organic ornament: rhythmic natural silhouettes and asymmetrical motifs.
- Plique-à-jour and other enamelling craft: see-through or opalescent colors framed by precise metal structures.

## Influenced To
- Contemporary digital material and decorative biomorphism as a plausible application, not an asserted individual historical lineage.

## Relations
- [Arkhip Kuindzhi](../kuindzhi_moonlit_dnieper/README.md): darkness makes select enamel light legible.
- [Yuri Norstein](../norstein_hedgehog_fog/README.md): overlapping translucent layers differ in media.

## Style Requirements
- **Enamel:** Organic foliage, fins or coral has luminous glass/enamel-like interior color with outlined compartments.
- **Iridescence:** At least two neighboring cool hues shift with angle or form across these surfaces.
- **Filigree:** Thin gold outlines or veins trace selected organic edges, not the entire scene.
- **Biomorphism:** Combine botanical and marine shapes into original fantasy life.
- **Pearl glints:** Small localized concentrated highlights imply gemstones or nacre.
- **Material distinction:** Jewelled fauna/flora visually contrast with matte rock and wood.
- **Originality:** Do not copy the distinctive dragonfly-woman or exact jewellery composition.
