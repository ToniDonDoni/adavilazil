# Karl Sims: Panspermia and Procedural Life

## Overview
Karl Sims created the computer animation Panspermia in 1990. According to Sims's own account, custom software, interactive selection among random mutations, procedural plant growth, simulated movement and particle systems were used to evoke self-propagating life. This entry extracts broad visual and conceptual principles of generative art, not the particular creatures, frames, algorithms or arrangements of the film. Backlog origin: FS-253.

## Influenced By
- Computer graphics and procedural modeling: programs supply generative rules rather than hand-placing every shape.
- Ideas from artificial evolution and biological propagation: variation, selection, repetition and transformation structure the subject.
- Physical simulation and particle animation: movement can arise from system behavior rather than scripted poses.

## Influenced To
- Generative-art and artificial-life practice: the project is an instructive historical example of simulated emergence, without asserting direct influence on individual later artists.

## Relations
- [Georg Nees: Schotter](../nees_schotter/README.md): both expose computational variation; Sims explores evolving organic structures rather than progressively scattered square outlines.

## Style Requirements
- **Emergence:** Make a visibly simple rule-based seed or structure develop into increasingly complex forms.
- **Branching:** Organize several invented forms into meaningful parent-child or recursive-looking relationships.
- **Variation:** Repeated units must vary perceptibly in size, direction and density instead of being copied identically.
- **Growth:** Provide an inspectable visual sequence or spatial progression from initiation to proliferation.
- **Complexity:** Allow clusters of local detail to emerge from a limited set of simple geometric primitives.
- **Nature:** Suggest speculative ecology or biological behaviors without reproducing Sims's specific life forms.
- **Motion cues:** In a static image, use directional traces, particles or repeated stages to imply propagation; do not falsely claim actual animation.
- **System visibility:** Make the relationship between an abstract underlying rule and resulting living form visually legible.
- **Contrast:** Separate one initiating element from its more complex descendants.
- **Originality:** Invent new morphology and topology; do not reproduce a Panspermia still or distinctive plant silhouette.

These are editorial constraints for a new still image, not claims that every Sims artwork has these properties.
