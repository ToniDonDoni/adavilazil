# Saul Leiter: Early Color Street Photography (late 1940s-1960s)

## Overview
Saul Leiter (1923-2013) began experimental color photography in New York in the late 1940s. This reference narrows to the *Early Color* urban language: scenes partially obscured by windows, frames, reflections, rain, haze and large patches of color. It is a photography method, not a generic orange-teal cinematic filter.

## Influenced By
- Painting and a lifelong painting practice: the Saul Leiter Foundation documents his continuing interest in painting and references Japanese woodblock and French impressionist art.
- New York street observation: ordinary pedestrians, vehicles and storefronts viewed as color and form rather than factual reportage alone.

## Influenced To
- Fine-art acceptance of expressive color street photography: Leiter's early experimental practice became widely recognized in retrospect.
- Contemporary editorial urban photography: partial visibility and foreground framing provide a useful creative reference, without asserting specific direct influence.

## Relations
- [Yuri Pimenov city painting](../yuri_pimenov_new_moscow/README.md): both value atmospheric street life, but Pimenov paints broad daylight and Leiter emphasizes chance occlusion and optical layering.
- [Edward Steichen Vogue portraits](../steichen_vogue_portraits/README.md): Leiter's incidental glimpses contrast with Steichen's controlled studio glamour.

## Style Requirements
- **Occlusion:** Include one purposeful foreground obstruction, e.g. dark window frame, translucent pane, or colored umbrella; do not cover the main face completely when portrait recognition is required.
- **Reflection:** Reflect or refract at least one distinct urban form in a semi-transparent plane, rather than adding random fog.
- **Color:** Use spatially important muted reds and warm/cool color fields; do not require universal saturation or one fixed LUT.
- **Crop:** Favor asymmetry, partial architecture, and off-center framing; some city content can remain undisclosed.
- **Focus:** Mix selective focus with soft background and readable near-field anchor.
- **Human scale:** Ordinary urban life may appear as small or partially hidden figures in the frame.
- **Medium:** Preserve photographic light, glass, grain and depth; avoid turning all surfaces into oil paint in the independent study.
- **Legibility:** Maintain enough image information to discover the subject even with deliberate ambiguity.
