# German Expressionist Poster

## Overview
A poster interpretation of early 20th-century German Expressionism, especially the Die Brücke artists' expressive woodcuts. The visual language reduces and distorts familiar forms to convey psychological pressure. Its woodcut and ink effects here are reproducible digital interpretations.

## Influenced By
- Vincent van Gogh and Edvard Munch: expressive distortion, intensity and psychological subject matter.
- German Gothic and Renaissance woodcuts: carved edges, stark contrasts and simplified forms.

## Influenced To
- Later expressive printmaking and poster illustration: the visual vocabulary of forceful cuts, emotional distortion and high-contrast imagery.

## Relations
- [Psychedelic poster](../psychedelic_poster/README.md): both distort familiar shapes to heighten emotion; jagged cuts contrast with fluid curves.
- [Soviet Constructivist poster](../soviet_poster/README.md): both use strong diagonals and graphic reduction, but for different expressive purposes.

## Style Requirements
- **Light:** Show large, hard-edged shadow masses against exposed highlights.
- **Color:** Restrict the composition to black, paper tones and no more than one prominent accent.
- **Form:** Visibly angularize or exaggerate at least one recognizable subject.
- **Carved line:** Use irregular, wedge-like strokes within the subject, not only around it.
- **Space:** Tilt or compress a recognizable plane, street or horizon.
- **Composition:** Use a dominant diagonal that pushes the scene off balance.
- **Print texture:** Show uneven ink coverage or woodcut-like surface marks.
- **Mood:** Create visual unease through shadow pressure, tilted space or an agitated pose.
