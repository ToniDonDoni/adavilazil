# Levitan-Inspired Watercolor

## Overview
A contemporary watercolor interpretation of Isaac Levitan's atmospheric Russian landscape art from the late 19th century. Quiet light and a sense of place establish the mood. Transparent washes and wet-on-wet textures are **modern techniques used to recreate the atmosphere**, not techniques Levitan is claimed to have invented.

## Influenced By
- Alexei Savrasov: a lyrical approach to the Russian landscape and attention to nature's emotional character.
- Vasily Polenov: further landscape and watercolor instruction during Levitan's training.

## Influenced To
- Later mood-centered Russian landscape study: a model for treating light, season and open space as carriers of emotion.

## Relations
- [Psychedelic poster](../psychedelic_poster/README.md): opposite in color intensity, but both can create an emotional experience through the treatment of space and atmosphere.
- [German Expressionist poster](../german_poster/README.md): compare quietly observed space with heightened, angular psychological pressure.

## Style Requirements
- **Light:** Keep daylight or dusk diffuse, with no sharply defined cast shadow.
- **Palette:** Let muted blue-grays and earth greens dominate the scene.
- **Distance:** Fade contrast and edges toward the far horizon.
- **Contours:** Avoid hard, continuous outlines around distant landscape forms.
- **Focus:** Sharpen only a few chosen foreground details.
- **Space:** Leave a visibly broad, quiet sky, river or lake area.
- **Watercolor:** Show translucent, overlapping pigment washes with soft transitions.
- **Paper:** Preserve a visible but subdued paper surface.
- **Mood:** Suggest stillness or melancholy with calm light, sparse detail and open space.
