# Arkhip Kuindzhi: Moonlit Night on the Dnieper

## Overview
Arkhip Kuindzhi's *Moonlit Night on the Dnieper* (1880), held by the State Russian Museum, uses dominant nocturnal darkness, a concentrated cool-green luminous region, and a subdued landscape. Backlog FS-153. This entry extracts broad visual relationships; it does not reconstruct the painting.

## Influenced By
- Nineteenth-century Romantic landscape painting: light and landscape as emotional subjects (broad visual context, not a claimed direct causal influence).

## Influenced To
- Digital light-led landscape studies: the following techniques are useful for adaptation, without claiming historical direct influence on a particular artist.

## Relations
- [René Lalique](../lalique_dragonfly_jewellery/README.md): bright material vs luminous atmosphere.
- [Yuri Norstein](../norstein_hedgehog_fog/README.md): selective visibility and dark space, through different media.

## Style Requirements
- **Darkness:** Blue-black areas occupy most of the visual field, with objects not evenly illuminated.
- **One focal source:** A singular cool green-white light remains visibly brightest.
- **Light path:** A connected tapering cool reflection or beam reaches into the surrounding environment.
- **Layering:** Separate dark foreground, subdued middle region and hazy distance.
- **Negative space:** Leave breathing room around the focal region without competing light sources.
- **Warm restraint:** Sparse warm gold is secondary to the cool focal light.
- **Originality:** Invent a new environment, not a copy of the river, coast or sky of this painting.
