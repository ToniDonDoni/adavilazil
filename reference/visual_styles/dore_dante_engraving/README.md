# Gustave Dore: Dante Engraving and the Sublime

## Overview
Gustave Dore (1832-1883) designed illustrations for Dante's Inferno, published in 1861, and Purgatorio and Paradiso, published in 1868. His designs were reproduced by wood engravers. Monumental imagined landscapes, minute human figures, bright openings within darkness, and carefully graduated engraved tones let a reader explore a world beyond ordinary scale. This entry focuses on general, observable principles, not a reconstruction of any particular illustration. Backlog origin: FS-065.

## Influenced By
- Romantic and sublime landscape traditions: visual drama derives from extreme scale, light, and apparently boundless space.
- Literary illustration and nineteenth-century wood engraving: narrative visions are translated into reproducible black-and-white tonal images.

## Influenced To
- Later fantasy and literary illustration: Dore's epic visual language became an enduring reference for depicting speculative worlds, without implying that all later fantasy imagery derives from him.

## Relations
- [Piranesi's Carceri](../piranesi_carceri/README.md): both use overwhelming scale and complex depth; Dore's chosen series depicts literary worlds rather than Piranesi's architectural prisons.

## Style Requirements
- **Scale:** Make a small human observer or explorer visibly subordinate to an immense environment.
- **Depth:** Distinguish close, intermediate and remote regions using overlap, size and tonal separation.
- **Light:** Organize the image around one identifiable bright opening or illuminated region within deep dark areas.
- **Tonal structure:** Establish dark masses and pale paths with intermediate values, rather than uniform gray.
- **Linework:** Use directional engraved-line or hatch-like texture on selected surfaces, with visibly varied density.
- **Atmosphere:** Let light and shadow establish distance and a sense of mystery; avoid a uniformly sharp scene.
- **Narrative:** Give the explorer a clear path, threshold or discovery to direct attention through the scene.
- **Composition:** Use dramatic asymmetry or diagonal movement, but do not reproduce a specific Dante illustration.
- **Surface:** Preserve a print-like black-and-white drawing language rather than photographic realism.
- **Originality:** Invent wholly new terrain, structures, figures and viewpoints; do not trace an existing plate.

These are testable adaptation guidelines, not assertions that Dore prescribed a fixed ruleset. The wood engravings were made after his designs; do not misdescribe them as personally carved plates.
