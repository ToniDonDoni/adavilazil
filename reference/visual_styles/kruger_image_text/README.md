# Kruger Image-Text Montage

## Overview
A picture-and-slogan idiom developed by Barbara Kruger from the early 1980s: a black-and-white photograph overlaid with a short declarative phrase set in white bold italic type inside solid red rectangles. The words sit on the image rather than beside it, so the photograph reads as evidence and the caption as verdict. *Untitled (Your body is a battleground)*, made in 1989 for the Women's March on Washington, is the canonical example: a face split into positive and negative halves, crossed by two red bars carrying a single sentence.

## Influenced By
- Advertising and tabloid layout: the direct address, the slogan format and the imperative tone borrowed from commercial media.
- Conceptual and Pictures Generation practice: the critique of images through images, with text as the carrier of meaning.
- Photomontage traditions: the deliberate collision of photograph and typeset message in one plane.

## Influenced To
- Protest graphics and campaign posters: a reusable formula for image plus short political statement.
- Editorial and social-media activism formats: red-bar captions over photographs became a widely imitated template.

## Relations
- [Fukuda negative space poster](../fukuda_negative_space/README.md): both reduce the message to one image and one line, but Kruger adds a photographic layer and speaks in a declarative voice instead of a visual pun.
- [Soviet Constructivist poster](../soviet_poster/README.md): both use bold type as an equal compositional element; Kruger keeps the photograph intact where Constructivism fragments and re-crops it.

## Style Requirements
- **Image layer:** Use a black-and-white photograph only; no color photography and no illustration in the photographic layer.
- **Text bars:** Place the phrase in white bold italic sans-serif inside one or more solid red rectangles.
- **Overlap:** Let at least one bar cross the subject, cropping the face or body rather than sitting in a margin.
- **Voice:** Write a short present-tense declaration addressed to a viewer ("you", "we", an imperative); no description or narration.
- **Palette:** Restrict the whole image to red, white and the greys of the photograph.
- **Type treatment:** Set the phrase tight, with minimal leading, no letterspacing, no outline and no drop shadow.
- **Framing:** Crop close to the subject so the face or body fills the frame and the words become the second subject.
- **Composition:** Keep a strong vertical axis and a near-symmetrical balance; the layout should look asserted, not arranged.
- **Tone split (optional):** Split the photograph into positive and negative halves, or repeat it inverted, to make the division literal.
- **Surface:** Keep everything flat and silkscreen-like; no gradients, texture or photographic depth effects.
