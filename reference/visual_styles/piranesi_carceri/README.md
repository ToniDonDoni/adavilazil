# Piranesi's Carceri d'invenzione

## Overview
Giovanni Battista Piranesi's Carceri d'invenzione (Imaginary Prisons) is a series of architectural fantasy etchings, not a depiction of real prisons. The first edition appeared in 1749-1750 as fourteen prints. Piranesi reworked the plates and issued a darker, more elaborate sixteen-print edition in 1761. The series builds immense, stage-like interiors from arches, vaults, staircases, bridges, galleries, beams and machinery. Low viewpoints, multiple vanishing points and tiny human figures make the spaces feel both monumental and claustrophobic. The deeper etched lines and stronger tonal contrasts of the 1761 edition are especially characteristic.

## Influenced By
- Classical Roman architecture and antiquities: monumental vocabulary of arches, vaults, columns and massive masonry.
- Theatrical stage design: constructed interiors, staged viewpoints and dramatic spatial effects rather than literal architectural plans.

## Influenced To
- M. C. Escher and Surrealist artists: later explorations of labyrinthine, paradoxical and impossible architecture.

## Relations
- German Expressionist poster: both use distortion and high contrast to create psychological pressure; Carceri achieves this through monumental architecture and etched chiaroscuro rather than angular figure distortion.

## Style Requirements
- **Architecture:** Build an immense, multi-level interior from massive piers, vaults, arches, staircases, bridges, galleries and platforms.
- **Perspective:** Combine a low viewpoint with multiple competing vanishing points. Allow spatial contradictions instead of forcing a coherent architectural plan.
- **Scale:** Let the architecture dominate. Use small, sparse human figures only as scale cues.
- **Depth:** Layer foreground obstructions, intermediate walkways and distant openings or stairways. Avoid giving the viewer a clear map or obvious exit.
- **Machinery:** Include ropes, chains, pulleys, levers, wheels or heavy beams as parts of the prison architecture, not as unrelated decoration.
- **Line:** Use etched and engraved marks, irregular hatching and scraped highlights. Avoid smooth digital gradients as the main shading method.
- **Light:** Create strong chiaroscuro with localized light sources and large shadow masses. The 1761 interpretation may use especially deep blacks and denser detail.
- **Mood:** Convey oppressive, uncanny vastness and uncertainty without relying on literal horror imagery.
